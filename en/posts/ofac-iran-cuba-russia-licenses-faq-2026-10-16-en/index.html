OFAC Issues New and Amended Licenses and FAQs Related to Iran, Cuba and Russia

On October 15, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department’s Office of Foreign Assets Control issued two Russia-related general licenses, one Iran-related general license, a new Cuba-related FAQ and an amended Russia-related FAQ.

2026-11-01 4 min read Share

On October 15, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department’s Office of Foreign Assets Control issued two Russia-related general licenses, one Iran-related general license, a new Cuba-related FAQ and an amended Russia-related FAQ. Businesses should verify the full text and conditions before treating any affected transaction as authorized.

Key developments

On October 15, 2026, the U.S. Treasury Department’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued a set of Russia- and Iran-related general licenses and Cuba- and Russia-related Frequently Asked Questions. The supplied official source identifies the documents and their titles, but it does not provide the full license text or the substantive question-and-answer content. Companies should therefore not determine the legality or scope of a transaction from a document title alone. [S1]

Documents issued

According to the source, OFAC issued the following documents:

  • Russia-related General License 128D, “Authorizing Certain Transactions Involving Lukoil Retail Service Stations Located Outside of Russia.”
  • Russia-related General License 130B, “Authorizing Transactions Involving Certain Lukoil Entities in Bulgaria.”
  • Iran-related General License FF, “Authorizing the Wind Down of Transactions Involving International Development Bank for Investment and Finance.”
  • New Cuba-related FAQ 1276.
  • Amended Russia-related FAQ 1225. [S1]

The topic of the OFAC notice refers to Iran-related designations and a Cuba designation. However, the supplied fact set does not describe the targets, legal basis, scope or changes associated with those designations. It also does not state the answer provided in new FAQ 1276 or identify the precise language changed in amended FAQ 1225.

Operational impact for businesses

The issuance gives companies a reason to revisit their sanctions-screening records, document versions and transaction classifications. Businesses handling Russia-related transactions can check whether a transaction involves a Lukoil retail service station located outside Russia or a certain Lukoil entity in Bulgaria. The titles state that certain transactions are authorized, but the supplied source does not identify which transactions qualify or what conditions apply. A company should not assume that every payment, shipment, service, insurance arrangement, brokerage activity or other transaction stage is covered.

Businesses reviewing Iran-related transactions can check whether a counterparty or transaction structure involves International Development Bank for Investment and Finance. General License FF is described as authorizing the wind down of transactions involving that institution. The source does not provide a wind-down period, an end date, eligible transaction types, permitted participants or other conditions. Those points must not be inferred from the title.

Companies with Cuba- or Russia-related compliance processes should distinguish the new FAQ 1276 from the amended FAQ 1225 in their records. The source confirms that FAQ 1276 is new and FAQ 1225 is amended, but it does not establish that either FAQ replaces a general license or independently authorizes all transactions.

Practical checks before processing

Companies can use the following checks as a controlled review step:

  • Confirm that General Licenses 128D, 130B and FF are included in the relevant OFAC document set.
  • For Russia-related transactions, compare the transaction documents with the location of any Lukoil retail service station and determine whether it is outside Russia.
  • Check whether a transaction involves a certain Lukoil entity in Bulgaria, while recognizing that the supplied source does not list the entities or define the relevant transaction scope.
  • For Iran-related activity, check for a direct or structural connection to International Development Bank for Investment and Finance.
  • Record FAQ 1276 as the new Cuba-related FAQ and FAQ 1225 as the amended Russia-related FAQ.
  • Do not classify a transaction as authorized solely because its facts resemble the subject described in a license title.
  • Review the full document text and applicable conditions before releasing goods, payments or services under an authorization theory.

Timing and unresolved points

The source publication date is October 15, 2026. It does not state a separate effective date, expiration date, retroactive application rule or transition period for any of the licenses or FAQs. As of October 16, 2026, businesses processing current, planned or historical transactions should therefore avoid relying on the publication date alone to determine whether a transaction is permitted.

The supplied material does not establish the detailed scope of the Iran-related designations, the extent of any Cuba-related designation, the interpretation provided by either FAQ, or the complete list of permitted and prohibited activities under the three general licenses. Where a transaction touches one of the named institutions, entities or facilities, the company should verify the original OFAC document and reassess the parties, location, timing, transaction type and internal approval record before proceeding.
